02 / Who does what

Four clear responsibilities.

Each person owns a different part of the same comparison. “Teammate 1–3” are assignment slots; Sean can add names when distributing the work.

S

Sean

Overall Model and Training Lead

What to do

  • Choose and approve the model versions used by everyone.
  • Supervise any model training or fine-tuning: what data is used, how it is checked, and which model is accepted.
  • Approve the selected clips, correction limits, and comparison rules.
  • Review failures and decide whether the result is ready to expand.

What to hand over

An approved model/version list, a short experiment plan, and the final accept-or-revise decision.

Days 1–2: approve setup · Day 5: review · Day 10: decide
1

Teammate 1

Baseline, comparison & evidence

What to do

  • Prepare the same existing reconstruction files for the whole team.
  • Check that the people, objects and video frames line up correctly.
  • Rebuild the existing starting result and smoothed result.
  • Run all comparisons, measure changes, and make matched before/after videos.
  • Check that the saved results reopen correctly and contain every required frame.

What to hand over

A shared input list, one comparison table, per-frame measurements, 4 comparison videos and 4 screenshot sheets.

Day 2: baseline ready · Day 5: first comparison · Day 10: evidence package
2

Teammate 2

Video review & contact evidence

What to do

  • Watch all 4 selected clips and check what each hand is doing.
  • Mark when hands touch, hold, slide and release; mark uncertainty when hidden.
  • Identify whether a problem comes from the hand, arm, object position, or object shape.
  • Prepare the hand-surface and object-distance information needed by Teammate 3.
  • Recommend which parts may be adjusted, and ask Sean to approve the limits.

What to hand over

Contact labels for both hands in 4 clips, a short problem list, and 4 approved correction plans.

Day 2: contact labels · Day 5: geometry checks · Day 8: interaction review
3

Teammate 3

Contact improvement & integration

What to do

  • First read and save the existing result without changing it.
  • Add improvements one at a time: steadier motion, less overlap between hands and objects, better touching, then better contact over time.
  • Keep stable holds together, allow sliding, and remove the hold constraint when the person lets go.
  • Keep the comparison settings consistent and record failures and running time.
  • Connect the accepted version to the existing pipeline and keep a fallback.

What to hand over

One configurable improvement tool, its result files and settings, timing logs, and an export check.

Fix the first problems.

DAYS 6–8

Make contact behave.

T3 adds touching and contact over time.
T2 checks holds and release.

DAYS 9–10

Compare and decide.

T2 explains remaining failures.
T3 freezes the tested version.

For this first pilot, keep body shape, object shape and size, and camera settings fixed. If these inputs are wrong, return them for correction before comparing methods.

For C–F, keep the same inputs, adjustable parts, correction limits, starting values and compute budget. E/F use the same contact labels. Existing weak-contact code can be an additional comparison on supported cases; extra runs are outside the 24 core bundles.

Measure: mean, p95 and maximum penetration; contact gap; sliding during a stable hold; agreement with the image; action timing; runtime, memory use and failures.

Public Track 1 does not provide the reconstruction reference trajectories needed for official CD/ACC evaluation. Local acceleration and penetration checks must be labeled as diagnostics or proxies. Do not use Track 2 assets or derivatives.

What must be true before Sean accepts the result?
  • Complete output: preserve 100% of required original frame IDs and export valid human parameters and object rotations.
  • Faithful to the video: better contact must not come from moving the hand away, moving the object away from where the video shows it, or changing the action timing.
  • Agreed tolerances: Sean freezes numerical quality limits after baseline review on Day 2, before selecting candidates.
  • Reproducible evidence: record input hashes, model versions, settings, allowed corrections, runtime and failure cases. Another teammate must reproduce the local report.
  • Honest uncertainty: hidden hands and inaccurate object geometry may remain unresolved. Reject or investigate bad repairs; retain the baseline as fallback.
  • Controlled expansion: first check complete EP00/EP12/EP16 (1,555 frames), including boundaries and unchanged regions; then consider all 30 episodes (16,563 frames).

Handoffs: T1 shares frozen inputs with everyone. T2 gives contact evidence and approved correction plans to T3. T3 returns candidates and settings to T1. Sean reviews the combined evidence.

Use separate task branches and one primary editor for shared files. If the existing runtime or files cannot be restored, resolve that dependency before real-data optimization.
