1. What goes wrong in the foam-block grip?

In EP16, the person’s right hand holds a rotating foam block. The left hand is mostly hidden. In the starting reconstruction, parts of the hands pass through the block. The attempted repair draws the hands toward its surface under the assumption that both are grasping.

2. How to train a contact repair model

Give the model half a second of video and the existing 3D hand and object poses. Training member 1 teaches it to judge whether each hand touches the target. Training member 2 uses that judgment to make small wrist and finger adjustments. A good pose can pass through with zero change.

Prepare examples that another person can replay

Foundation team · Replay and measurement

Load the original video, the reconstructed hands, the object surface and the camera. Draw the starting poses over the image. Check that the same hand and object line up on the same original frame. For each hand, save a short crop that shows both it and the object, plus their 3D poses. About half a second (15 frames at 30 fps) is a starting window.

Assign whole objects to three groups before making crops. For the ten Track 1 objects, a proposed split is six for learning, two for choosing settings and two for the final test. Keep EP16 in development because it helped design the method. Record earlier use of any proposed test object.

Hand over a replayable baseline and a list linking every window to its object, episode, hand, original frame and data group.

Fill the gaps in the Track 1 labels

Foundation team · Video labeling, checked by the replay partner

EP16 gives us one ongoing right-hand hold and a hidden left hand. Use the camera view alone to find these missing events across Track 1 objects. Record the target object, the person’s left or right hand, and the original start and end frames for each interval.

  • Near miss. Mark a hand that approaches the target but leaves a visible gap as clear.
  • First touch. Record the first frame where contact is visible, then mark the touching interval. If the motion is clear, add holding or sliding.
  • Release. Record the first frame where a gap reappears. Include the clear frames afterward, even if the hand stays close.
  • Occlusion and reappearance. Mark the hidden interval cannot tell and label the first visible state when the hand returns. If the transition happened out of sight, record its possible frame range.

In a separate pass, overlay the starting 3D hands on the footage. Save plausible poses for the repair trainer only after checking image alignment, penetration into the object and fingers passing through each other. Exclude the failed v11 left-hand poses from those targets.

Hand over reviewed intervals, a count of distinct events per object and a list of usable pose targets. Keep unknown rows in the file and leave them out of touch supervision.

Teach the computer to spot a touch

Training member 1 · Contact recognition

For each hand, crop the hand and target together across 15 frames. Keep the pretrained image reader fixed. Train a small sequence model to predict the chance of touch at the middle frame, using the reviewed touching and clear labels. Give first touches, releases and near misses enough examples of their own.

On validation objects, count false touches, missed touches and how many frames early or late the model calls a release. Choose the model version and confidence cutoff there. Start with one decision per hand. Add hold versus slide, or finger and palm contact regions, when reviewed labels support that level of detail.

Give training member 2 the saved model, its cutoff, and a file listing the episode, original frame, hand, target, predicted state and confidence.

Train the model to correct the hand pose

Training member 2 · Pose repair and model lead

Start with a reviewed pose from the training group. Add a small, known wrist or finger error. Give the repair model this altered pose, the nearby object surface and the contact prediction. Its target is the joint adjustment that restores the reviewed pose. Also show unchanged poses with a target adjustment of zero. Generate predictions for each training object with a contact reader fitted to the other training objects. Keep the validation and test objects out of that fitting step.

Check that the model can undo one known error and leave a good grip alone. Then train on short sequences. Penalize wrong joint corrections, hand points entering the object and fingers crossing each other. Keep the camera and object motion fixed. Turn off the pull toward the object on release or when contact is uncertain. For EP16, this means the hidden left hand still gets a shape check. When hold and slide labels are available, steady a held contact point and let a sliding one travel along the surface.

Hand over repair weights and a repeatable run that saves corrected poses, same-frame overlays and a reason whenever it keeps the original pose.

ContactOpt is a research example of predicting likely contact regions and using them to refine a hand pose. Read the ContactOpt paper.

Test on real mistakes and decide what to adopt

Replay partner scores · Model lead signs off

Use natural errors on validation footage to choose movement limits. Before opening reserved test results, write down the required improvement and allowed changes in image fit, timing and geometry. Freeze both models and their settings. Run these comparisons on identical frames.
